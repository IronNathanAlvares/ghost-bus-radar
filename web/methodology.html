How it works

Every number on this site comes from two public NTA feeds: the timetable (GTFS) and the live feed (GTFS-Realtime), which we record every minute. Each scheduled trip gets exactly one outcome. Below is what each word means, which trips count, and what the numbers cannot tell you.

Trip outcomes

Rules are checked in this order; the first that fits decides.

Out of scope
Rail and Luas. The live feed has no vehicle positions for them, so a missing vehicle proves nothing. Never counted anywhere.
Cancelled with notice
Marked cancelled in the live feed at least 15 minutes before it was due to leave.
Cancelled late
Marked cancelled with less notice than that.
Ran
Tracked through at least 60% of its route's stops.
Vanished
Tracked on the route (it reached at least one stop), then lost before reaching 60%.
Phantom
Not cancelled and never reached a stop, but a vehicle was assigned: the board showed a live countdown to a bus that never came.
Ghost
Never tracked, never cancelled, no vehicle ever assigned.
No data
A gap in our own recording could have hidden what happened. Excluded from every number: our downtime is never counted as a missing bus.

Untracked = ghost + phantom: the bus never showed up on the live map at all. This is the headline number, because it is what a passenger experiences as "the bus on the board never came" with no warning.

Which trips count

Only fully collected bus trips: ones whose whole window, from 30 minutes before the scheduled start to 30 minutes after the scheduled end, falls inside our recording. Across a recording gap, buses that ran still show as ran, but buses that didn't would show as no data, so including those trips would make service look better than it was. Every figure states how many trips it is based on.

95% intervals

A share from a few dozen trips is uncertain, so every share comes with a 95% Wilson score interval: the range the true share very likely lies in. A route at "20%" from 10 trips has an interval of about 6% to 51%, which says much less than 20% from 1,000 trips.

Ranking routes

Routes are ranked by the lower end of the 95% interval of their untracked share, worst first, and only with at least 30 trips in the window. A route is near the top only when the data is confident it is bad, not when it had a few unlucky trips.

Quality checks and published days

Every night, automated checks look at the day just finished: how much of it we recorded, whether timetable and live trips matched up, whether the stored archive is intact, and more. A day that fails is listed in the data status table, but counted in no number on this site. A day passes with warnings when something looked unusual but not wrong.

The hour-by-day heatmap

Each square is one hour of one day type (weekdays, Saturdays, Sundays), by when trips were due to start, in Irish time. The further a square's colour is along the key, the larger the untracked share. Squares with fewer than 10 trips are hatched: too few to read anything into. Bank holidays count under their weekday.

Live data is the newest snapshot we recorded, at most a few minutes old. Expected times come from the delays in the live feed for that stop, or carried forward from the last earlier stop with a prediction.

The chance a bus comes

The chance that the trip runs (is tracked through most of its route), estimated from the same published history as the rest of this site, for that route at that day type and hour. What the live feed already shows is taken into account: a bus tracked live cannot be a ghost, so its chance is higher; a bus given a vehicle cannot be a ghost either; close to departure, a cancellation with notice would already have been announced. Hours with few trips lean on the route's overall record.

This is model heuristic-v1: built on stated assumptions, and checked every night against what actually happened, on days it was not built from: how good it has been so far. Read it as a guide, not a promise. When a route has no published history yet, we say so instead of showing a number.

How good is the chance?

Every night we check the chance against what happened, on days the model never saw. For each bus that day, we replay the live feed as it was 30, 15, 5 and 1 minutes before it was due, ask the model exactly as the "My stop" page would have, using only the weeks before, and compare with whether the bus ran.

The Brier score is the average squared gap between the chance and what happened (1 if it ran, 0 if not): 0 is perfect, and lower is better. We compare it with two simple rivals: "the bus always comes" (what a timetable implies) and one base rate for every bus. The chance is only worth showing if it beats both. The reliability diagram groups predictions by the chance given; if the chance is honest, about 80% of the buses given 80% really ran.

What the numbers can't tell you

  • "Untracked" means the live feed never saw the bus. A bus with a broken tracker that ran on time is counted as untracked. Spot checks at real stops are how we measure this.
  • Rail and Luas are not covered.
  • Extra buses added on the day, outside the timetable, are not shown at stops yet.
  • We are independent of the NTA and the operators; nothing here is official.
